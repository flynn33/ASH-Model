# ASH Leaf Branching: From a Finite Local Alphabet to Infinite Models

**Research note v0.1 — 23 October 2026**

## 1. Query Understanding

The goal is to make leaf branching a primary mathematical object of ASH, preserving the finite Enneahcube/code foundation while specifying genuinely infinite histories and, where desired, infinitely many distinguishable global configurations. The scope is foundational mathematics, candidate dynamics, cosmological interpretation, and verification criteria. This is not a repository audit, an implementation change, or an observational validation of ASH cosmology.

The Project's corrected branch roadmap already places the finite local alphabet and the unbounded branch structure alongside one another [P1]. This note supplies explicit constructions and identifies the additional assumptions needed to move from a history space to an infinite dynamical state space.

The main result is conditional but constructive: **a finite ASH alphabet can support a countably infinite prefix tree, an uncountable boundary of infinite histories, and a countably infinite family of future-distinguishable configurations without changing the local [9,4,4] code.** These are distinct meanings of infinity and should not be conflated.

## 3. Step-by-Step Reasoning

### 3.1 Definitions: labels, prefixes, leaves, and completed histories

Let A be a finite action alphabet. For a rooted, prefix-closed set T contained in A*, a node u = (a_1,...,a_n) is a finite history. Its depth is n. A local-label map lambda:T -> Omega assigns a finite ASH state to each history.

The depth-n frontier L_n consists of the admissible histories of length n. Such nodes are leaves of the depth-n truncation, not necessarily terminal leaves of the full tree. The infinite boundary is

    boundary(T) = {omega in A^N : every finite prefix of omega belongs to T}.

An infinite history is not an extra node reached after an infinite computation. It is a mathematically defined compatible sequence of finite prefixes.

Distinct prefixes may have the same local label. Identifying them solely by that label destroys their history identity. Conversely, different bookkeeping paths should not automatically be declared different physical worlds: a physical equivalence relation may identify some of them.

For a full b-ary tree with b >= 2, |L_n| = b^n. Its finite-prefix node set is countably infinite, while its infinite boundary has cardinality 2^(aleph_0). The countability follows from a countable union of finite levels. For the boundary, a binary infinite sequence embeds by choosing two symbols, and a finite-alphabet sequence can be encoded into a binary one. These cardinality arguments are mathematical deductions, not simulation results.

For a restricted finitely branching tree, arbitrarily deep admissible prefixes guarantee at least one infinite path by König's lemma [S6]. They do not, by themselves, guarantee uncountably many paths. Persistent independent splitting is an additional condition. Terminal paths require explicit handling, for example an absorbing terminal symbol when constructing a probability space.

### 3.2 A complete ASH-compatible history model

Choose A = C. Every history u has one child uc for each c in C. Set

    lambda(empty) = x_0,
    lambda(uc) = lambda(u) XOR c.

Hence

    lambda(c_1,...,c_n) = x_0 XOR c_1 XOR ... XOR c_n.

**Closure and sector invariant [LB2].** Since C is a linear subspace, every endpoint belongs to x_0 + C. If x_0 is in C, every endpoint is a valid codeword. There are 512/16 = 32 cosets, each containing 16 labels. Unlimited branching under these moves does not connect distinct cosets.

**Growth [LB3].** There are 16^n history leaves at depth n. For n >= 1 and any fixed endpoint y in x_0+C, the first n-1 moves can be chosen arbitrarily, and the last is uniquely determined. Thus each endpoint has exactly 16^(n-1) histories.

The program explicitly enumerated all histories at depths 1 through 4:

| Depth | Histories | Distinct endpoint labels | Histories per endpoint |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 16 | 16 | 1 |
| 2 | 256 | 16 | 16 |
| 3 | 4,096 | 16 | 256 |
| 4 | 65,536 | 16 | 4,096 |

This is a genuine infinite history model, but its endpoint dynamics alone remain finite. In the uniform version, choosing a fresh uniform codeword makes the next endpoint uniform over the same coset. Keeping an unused history log does not change those endpoint predictions.

This is deliberately a control model, not a claim that 16-way branching is ASH's physically correct grammar. It does not replace an existing six-generator rule or fix an implementation's branching factor.

### 3.3 A genuine infinite configuration model

To demonstrate more than the unfolding of a finite state machine, introduce a finite-but-unbounded stack m of codeword blocks. The global configuration is m in C*, with local label

    x(m) = XOR of all blocks in m; x(empty)=0.

Two kinds of actions are permitted:

    push(c): m -> m concatenated with c, for c in C;
    pop: remove the last block, permitted only when m is nonempty.

Every operation preserves membership of x(m) in C. A push toggles x by c; a pop toggles x by the removed block. At any finite step the stack is finite. Across all possible depths, the configuration space is countably infinite.

**Future-distinguishability proof [LB5].** Fix a nonzero g in C and let m_k consist of 2k copies of g. Every m_k has the same local label, zero. If k < ell, the action sequence consisting of 2k+1 pops is disallowed from m_k but allowed from m_ell. Therefore m_k and m_ell cannot be represented by the same exact dynamical state while preserving future action legality. Infinitely many such k exist. No finite-state representation can preserve that whole contract.

The argument holds for arbitrary k and ell; the executed tests checked 66 distinct pairs of even lengths from 2 to 24. Closure was checked for every stack of length at most 3: 4,369 states, 69,904 push transitions, and 4,368 pop transitions.

This construction is a witness of mathematical possibility, not a proposed physical stack inside the universe and not a novelty claim in automata theory. Its significance for ASH is that local code integrity can coexist with genuinely unbounded, behaviorally relevant memory. For a cosmological model, the corresponding memory could instead be a causal event structure.

### 3.4 A probability law that survives infinite depth

For each prefix u, choose nonnegative conditional probabilities p(a|u) summing to one over its allowed children. Define its cylinder probability by

    mu([a_1...a_n]) = product from k=1 to n of p(a_k | a_1...a_(k-1)).

Here [u] is the set of all infinite histories beginning with u. Necessarily,

    mu([u]) = sum over children a of mu([ua]).

This consistency condition makes the finite-prefix distributions marginals of a single measure. For the finite-alphabet setting, the Kolmogorov extension theorem supplies a unique measure on the sigma-algebra generated by cylinders [S1]. This statement does not require assigning a positive atom to every infinite path.

For the uniform code-move model, mu([u]) = 16^(-|u|). Every level has total probability one. Each particular infinite path has probability zero, while the entire boundary has probability one. The model is not formed by summing the masses of all nodes at all depths, which would count the same events repeatedly.

**A failed construction worth excluding [LB6].** Normalizing every depth independently is insufficient. At depth 1 give branches 0 and 1 masses 1/2 each. At depth 2 give 00,01,10,11 masses 1/8,1/8,3/8,3/8. Both levels sum to one, but the deeper marginal of branch 0 is 1/4, contradicting 1/2. There is no single measure with both as its prefix marginals. The test harness checks this exact rational counterexample.

Accordingly, an action-weighted expression exp(-S_n)/Z_n needs cross-depth consistency, a valid limiting construction, or an explicit finite-horizon interpretation. A local conditional rule proportional to exp(-incremental_cost(u,a)) is one possible normalized classical grammar; it is not automatically equivalent to a separately normalized Gibbs law at each horizon and is not a Born-rule derivation.

### 3.5 Entropy: unbounded histories without unbounded local information

In the uniform code-move example,

    H(history at depth n) = 4n bits,
    H(endpoint at depth n) = 4 bits, for n >= 1.

For a general consistent classical branching law, refining the history gives

    H_(n+1) - H_n = sum over u in L_n of mu([u]) H(p(.|u)) >= 0.

These statements concern Shannon uncertainty over histories. They do not prove thermodynamic entropy production, a physical arrow of time, horizon entropy, or a cosmological energy density. Even monotone history entropy can arise just because a longer sequence is being recorded.

An abstract branch-distance candidate is d(omega,eta)=2^(-r), where r is the common-prefix length, with d=0 for equal histories. This encodes common ancestry. It must not be identified with spatial distance or decoherence without another argument.

### 3.6 A cosmology-oriented extension: grow configurations inside branches

Keep two graphs separate:

- T: the tree of alternative histories;
- Gamma_u: the event/causal graph within the world described by prefix u.

A candidate universe configuration is Q_u=(Gamma_u, xi_u), where xi_u labels every event with a codeword. Each branch step may add an event, update labels, or change permitted relations according to a specified rule. The set of world configurations can then be unbounded even though every event has a finite local alphabet.

For a minimal algebraic growth candidate, choose a finite set P of existing parents and c in C, add a new event v, and assign

    xi(v) = (XOR over p in P of xi(p)) XOR c.

Closure follows from linearity. Directing new edges from existing parents to the newly created event prevents directed cycles. Those deductions do not establish physical locality, covariance, manifold-like geometry, conservation of energy, or observational viability. Rules for parent selection and physical equivalence are still needed.

Sequential causal-set growth is a relevant comparison family, not proof of ASH: Rideout and Sorkin explicitly distinguish the growing causal set from paths through a space of possible causets and impose covariance conditions to remove unphysical birth-label dependence [S3, Section 1.1 and Section 3]. ASH should likewise test whether nominally different histories differ physically or merely by event relabeling.

Branch depth is initially an update index. It is not automatically proper time. Leaf count is not spatial volume. An infinite ensemble of histories does not imply infinite spatial extent within any one history.

### 3.7 Quantum branching: a construction and its limitations

A concrete recorded-history construction is available. Let H_n have an orthonormal basis |u> indexed by depth-n prefixes. Define

    V_n |u> = sum over allowed a of sqrt(p(a|u)) exp(i theta(u,a)) |ua>.

Different parent histories have disjoint child labels. Consequently their images are orthogonal, while each image has squared norm sum_a p(a|u)=1. Therefore V_n^dagger V_n=I: this is an isometry. When the output dimension grows it is not a surjective unitary map between the two finite spaces. A closed quantum model needs a consistent larger-system treatment and an infinite-limit construction.

The tests verify one 16-to-256-dimensional instance. They also verify that normalized columns alone do not imply an isometry: the matrix with both columns equal to (1,0) sends a normalized equal superposition to a vector of squared norm 2.

Assigning phases to permanently separated history labels is not enough to demonstrate quantum behavior. If all operations and observables only inspect those labels, their probabilities coincide with the classical model. Phase-sensitive observables or interference dynamics must be specified. Where alternatives interfere, amplitudes add before probabilities are formed. Probabilities over coarse-grained quantum histories require the relevant consistency/decoherence conditions; Hartle gives the explicit framework and its interference counterexample [S2, Sections II and IV].

The standard classical extension theorem cannot simply be applied across incompatible quantum measurement protocols. Milz and collaborators develop the required generalized consistency framework for processes with interventions [S4, Sections 3–4].

The present classical code also does not, by itself, establish protection against arbitrary quantum errors. For example, a relative phase change between two valid codeword basis vectors does not change their classical code membership. Quantum error correction is a separate construction.

For an Everett-style interpretation, pruning in a numerical approximation must be distinguished from physical destruction of worlds. Observer records should be modeled within a branch rather than an unexplained global operation that selects one history. Other ontologies remain separate research candidates; none is established by branch cardinality.

### 3.8 Finite exploration, controls, and falsification

A mathematical infinite tree can be specified exactly while calculations use finite prefixes. L-system literature supplies a precedent for finite grammar specifications of recursive structures, but grammar alone supplies neither a quantum probability law nor a spacetime interpretation [S5, abstract only].

For classical numerical pruning, track original probability mass. If a set of retained histories has mass 1-delta and is renormalized, the resulting distribution differs from the original by total variation delta. For a bounded observable |f|<=M, the resulting expectation error is at most 2M delta. This is a direct decomposition argument: mu=(1-delta)mu_retained+delta mu_discarded. It does not give a quantum amplitude truncation bound or control arbitrary future-dependent quantities without further assumptions.

Use a prefix-free frontier when accounting for mass, so that a history and its descendants are not counted simultaneously as disjoint alternatives. Terminal, retained, and discarded cylinders must form the intended partition.

The full-code uniform branching baseline is generic to a 16-element binary additive group. Any rank-4 binary code is abstractly isomorphic to F_2^4, so the history count and uniform endpoint law cannot demonstrate an ASH-specific benefit. Geometry-aware transitions, defect penalties, decoding, or inter-event constraints must make testable use of the actual code embedding if an ASH-specific effect is claimed.

An outcome reproduced by those controls does not invalidate the construction, but removes ASH-specific attribution for that outcome.

## 5. Final Answer

The next phase should develop **ASH Leaf-Branch Calculus v0.1**, making four objects explicit: the prefix tree, the infinite history boundary, the within-branch global configuration, and the probability or quantum process law.

Start with the uniform invariant tree as an exact baseline, then develop unbounded-memory and growing-event-graph candidates. Keep the quantum branch as a separate, equally explicit research track rather than relabeling classical weights as amplitudes. The finite and infinite parts belong in the same model from the start.

The first research milestone is: **one complete branch rule with local closure, a well-defined infinite limit, behaviorally meaningful branch identity, a normalized classical measure or justified quantum process, and at least one observable capable of distinguishing it from the finite baseline.**

## Appendix B. Search, contradictions, exclusions, and gaps

Discovery searched tree existence, cylinder measures, decoherent histories, and L-system grammar. Targeted searches located primary/author-hosted sources and the original causal-set growth comparison. The challenge pass examined mathematical counterexamples: equal endpoint labels, inconsistent horizon normalization, normalized but nonorthogonal transition columns, and history counts reproduced by generic controls. These were internal checks, not an independent reviewer or experiment.

Secondary encyclopedia results, aggregators, and mirrored abstracts were discovery aids rather than relied-upon technical evidence. Related causal-set follow-up papers were discovered but not reviewed deeply. The Quantum publisher page initially timed out; the authors' arXiv full-text HTML was successfully inspected instead.

The note qualifies three overstrong readings of earlier project material: per-parent squared-amplitude normalization is not general unitarity; many-worlds ontology does not follow from a tree; and branch entropy does not itself establish thermodynamic or cosmological entropy. All claims of physical geometry, Born-rule derivation, and empirical advantage remain unresolved.

Excluded from this scoped pass: live repository inspection, package modification, observational data analysis, exhaustive literature review, quantum gravity derivation, and performance benchmarking. Their exclusion does not imply impossibility; they require specified dynamics and observables beyond this initial construction.
